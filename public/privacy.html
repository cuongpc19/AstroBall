Privacy Policy — AstroBall

Last updated: 14 August 2026

AstroBall is a puzzle game. This page explains exactly what the game records, why, and what it never touches. It is short because the game collects very little.

What we do not collect

No name, no email address, no phone number, no location, no contacts, no photos, and no advertising identifiers. The game has no accounts and no login of its own, and never asks you for personal details.

Gameplay statistics

When a level starts, and again when it ends, the game sends one short record so we can tell which levels are too hard or too easy and rebalance them. The record sent at the start carries only the level number, its design fingerprint, and the device code below — it is what lets us see that a level was reached but never finished. The record sent at the end contains the level number and a fingerprint of that level's design, whether you won or lost, how long the attempt took, how many trays you tipped, how full the conveyor ever got, the stars awarded, which boosters the attempt used, a random device code, the platform and game version, and the name of the site the game was played on.

One more record is sent when you collect a daily login reward: which day of the streak it was, the coins and magnets it paid, the level you have reached, and the same device code, platform and version as above. It tells us how many players find the reward at all. It is not tied to any account and carries nothing you typed.

The record also contains the moves you made in that level — which trays you tapped and in what order, when each ball reached the conveyor, and any boosters or undos, all measured in the game’s own clock ticks rather than in real time. This lets us play a lost attempt back and see why a level is too hard, instead of only knowing that it was. It is a list of taps on a game board and nothing else: no text, no input from your keyboard, and nothing about anything outside the level.

The device code is generated at random and is not derived from anything about you or your device. It is not linked to any identity and cannot be used to contact or recognise you anywhere else. Clearing your browser storage erases it, and a new unrelated code is created next time.

Where it is stored

These records are stored in Google Firebase Realtime Database and are used only to balance the game's difficulty. They are never sold, rented, or shared with advertisers or any third party.

Saved progress

Your level progress, coins, stars and settings are stored on your own device, in your browser's local storage, under keys beginning with bf_. When you play on CrazyGames, that same data is also handled by the CrazyGames Data Module so your progress can follow your CrazyGames account between devices. That part is governed by CrazyGames' own privacy policy.

Cookies and analytics

Once you reach a level, the game loads Google Analytics to count how many people are playing and roughly where from. It records that a level started and ended, the level number, and whether it was won — nothing about you. Google derives an approximate country from your IP address and sets its own cookies for this; the IP address itself is not stored by us. The game runs no advertising trackers of its own. Sites that host the game, such as CrazyGames, may set their own cookies and show their own advertising — see their policy, linked above. Blocking these scripts does not affect how the game plays.

Children

The game is suitable for a general audience and does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children. Since no personal information is collected at all, there is nothing about a child to store.

Your choices

  • Clearing your browser's site data removes your saved progress and your device code.
  • Playing with browser storage blocked stops progress being saved at all.
  • To ask what is stored under a device code, or to have it deleted, contact us below.
