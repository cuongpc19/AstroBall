A/B test — hai nhánh cạnh nhau

Mỗi chỉ số có hai cột kề nhau: A là bản đang chạy, B là biến thể. User start là số người vào màn (mỗi người tính một lần), Lượt vào là số lần vào — chia hai số đó ra là số lần thử lại mỗi người. Ván là số ván kết thúc, User xong là số người từng thắng màn này. Win/ván = thắng ÷ Lượt vào; Win/người = User xong ÷ User start. ⚠ Ô mờ nghĩa là dưới 8 ván nên chưa đọc được — median của một hai ván là chính một hai ván đó, không phải đặc điểm của màn. Level ngoài 3, 4, 5 là cùng một bàn cờ ở hai nhánh, nên chênh lệch ở đó gần như luôn là mẫu nhỏ.

Reading the columns

Winrate — here, in the by-day table and on the cards at the top — runs low to high: under 30%, 30-50%, 50-70%, 70-90%, 90%+, grey = fewer than 8 games, which is too few to mean anything. ⚠ Green is high, not correct: the ladder aims each level at its own target, so a green level well above target is a level that got easier than it was meant to be.

All three winrate columns are completed ÷ started, so an attempt someone walked away from counts against the level — it used to be counted over finished attempts only, which quietly deleted every abandonment from the maths and flattered exactly the levels people were giving up on. They differ in who is counted: Winrate/game = every attempt begun. Winrate/player = players who ever cleared it ÷ players who ever started it. Winrate_c = the same as /game but counting only the players who cleared it in the end, so it is roughly how many goes it takes someone who can do it: 50% is two attempts, 33% is three. ⚠ It is not a success rate — by construction everyone in it succeeded — and it can only fall as people retry. ⚠ Read together they separate two different faults. Level 10 currently reads 48% / 85% / 73%: most attempts end in failure, yet nearly everyone gets there, taking about one and a half goes. A level where all three sag is a different problem from one where only the first does.

⚠ Only attempts the log can pair are counted — an end row whose start row is missing is dropped from both sides. Builds before start-logging sent no start rows, so their games are absent from every winrate here (they still count in Games, Completed and Starts (user)). Dividing raw wins by raw starts instead would put level 1 at 169%. Starts (event) = how many times this board was entered; a player who retried it five times is five. Starts (user) = how many distinct people pressed start on it. Both count the same rows — one per attempt, one per device — so event ÷ user is retries per player, and event can never come out below user. Both include people who arrived and walked away without finishing, which is what makes them the pair to read Quit against. ⚠ Both show —, never 0, before start logging existed (first start row: 19/08). Those players did press start; the build simply never said so, and printing 0 beside a hundred finished games would be a claim rather than a gap. Their games still count in Games, Completed and Winrate/player, which are built from end rows. ⚠ Starts (user) used to be the union of everyone seen at the level, inferring a start from any finished attempt so that old builds were not lost. That number disagreed with the column’s own name and with Starts (event) beside it — level 1 read 318 events against 561 people — and there was no way to tell arrivals that were counted from arrivals that were inferred. Won & left still uses the seen-anywhere test, deliberately: an old-build player who finished the next level plainly went there. % of L1 = this row's Starts (user) divided by level 1's — the funnel, in the two numbers you can check by hand on the row itself, and blank for windows with no start rows in them. ⚠ It is a ratio of two headcounts, not a cohort: someone on level 30 today mostly started level 1 weeks ago, so a short window counts them here and not at level 1, and the column can go over 100% — highlighted when it does, because that is the shape of the window rather than a board more popular than the first. ⚠ For the same reason it rises as the window shortens, which is the opposite of what a funnel should do; read it on 7 or 30 days, where most of the people on a late board did start level 1 inside it. Hover the cell for the cohort reading — of the people who started level 1 inside this window, how many reached this board, and how many of the players here began before the window and so are in the ratio's top half only. Level 1 is 100% by construction; <1% means somebody got there and whole percent would have rounded them away, which is not the same as 0%. Blank until level 1 has at least 8 players in the window. Completed = how many of those players ever cleared it. Gave up = players who started the level, never won it, and never opened the next one within ten minutes of that start — the ones who stopped playing here rather than moved on. Not the same as Quit (one abandoned attempt) or Won & left (they beat it and never came back). Starts inside the last ten minutes of the window are excluded rather than counted, since they have not had their ten minutes yet; the hover says how many. Both timestamps come from the same device, so a wrong device clock cancels out here.

Lost or retried = distinct players the board gave trouble to — they lost at least once, or re-entered it, or started it more than twice — counted once however many of the three they hit; % of starts is that over Starts (user). ⚠ The log has no retry event, so a retry is inferred from a second start row on the same board. That makes "re-entered" and "more than twice" the same signal at two strengths, and the hover splits all three so you can see which is carrying the number. ⚠ The price of inferring it: a page reload re-enters the board, and so does coming back from an ad — neither is a retry the player would recognise, and both land here. ⚠ It is not the opposite of Completed and the two overlap deliberately: somebody who failed twice and then cleared the board is in both. That is what makes the pair worth reading together — a board where nearly everyone loses once and nearly everyone finishes is a board that teaches, while the same Lost beside half the Completed is a wall. Hover the cell for the split and for how many never cleared it. ⚠ Counted over the start-logged players only — the same population the denominator counts, or the percentage could print over 100. ⚠ Uncoloured on purpose — the ladder deliberately aims some levels as low as 25%, so players losing is the design working, not a fault. ⚠ — where there are no start rows to divide by, the same convention as the columns beside it. Quit = attempts that reached the board and never came back with a result — someone who walked away mid-level. ⚠ It is a percentage of Starts (event), not of Starts (user). Won & left = players who cleared this level and were never seen at the next one. They did not leave because it was too hard, so this is the churn difficulty cannot explain. ⚠ Read the last few rows with suspicion: the newest level in the window has no next level to be absent from, and someone who cleared a board minutes ago has not had time to start the one after it — both push this number up on their own. Drop-off = players who started this level and never started the next one. % drop by lose splits that into the part difficulty explains: of the players who left after this board, how many had just lost. The rest had just won it and stopped anyway. ⚠ Two levels with the same Drop-off can be opposite problems — a high number here is a board beating people, a low one is churn the board cannot be blamed for and cannot fix. ⚠ It counts each player's last attempt, so someone who lost twice and then cleared it counts as a winner who left; they were not beaten. ⚠ The hover gives its own base, which is the real set of people who left; the Drop-off percentage beside it is a ratio of two headcounts, so the two are not meant to reconcile by hand. ⚠ Under 8 leavers it shows the raw pair instead, greyed — 4/6, not 67%. Its base is a set difference and is far smaller than any other column’s, so on a short window most rows sit below any sane threshold; blanking them made the table read as though nobody ever left after losing, when the counts were there all along. Time is the median finished attempt on this board — half the attempts were quicker, half slower — not the average. ⚠ The mean is the wrong middle here: an attempt is timed until the player finishes it, so somebody who leaves the tab sitting on the board writes a half-hour game, and a handful of those move a level's average and not its median. The mean is still in the cell's hover, with how many attempts went into the figure; the two far apart is itself the finding. ⚠ It counts every finished attempt, won or lost, and counts attempts rather than players — someone who took four goes is in it four times. Attempts nobody came back from (the Quit column) report no length at all, so they are in neither number. Peak belt is how full the conveyor ever got, averaged — the thing stars are scored on, so a level near 100% is one nobody has slack on.
